Four rules

What the data says to change on Monday

Rule 01

Screen on views, never followers

Ask for the last five posts before you ask their rate. A 67K creator delivered 204,900 views at $1.46 CPM; a 2.5M creator delivered 2,256 views at $132.98. Both were beauty posts in the same quarter.

Rule 02

Ignore engagement rate as a value signal

Engagement per view runs against reach in your data — comment rate correlates +0.45 with a worse CPM, because small posts have concentrated ratios. Use engagement to check an audience is real, never to rank who to book.

Rule 03

Facebook is the opposite market

On TikTok, big accounts under-deliver. On Facebook they do not: 1M+ pages ran $1.31 CPM with zero blow-ups, against $35.32 for pages under 100K. Buy small on TikTok and big on Facebook.

Rule 04

Always contract the cross-post

Where a creator posted to both platforms, Facebook added a median 109% on top of TikTok reach for the same fee. Sixty-two of your 105 deals did this; the rest left that reach on the table.

Methodology

How the number is built

Forecast reach

Median views across the recent posts you enter, multiplied by the sponsored reach factor (default 0.75). Median resists a single viral post. The 0.75 is a convention, not something your file can prove — it holds no organic baseline for the same creators, so it is exposed as a dial. Where a Facebook cross-post is contracted, forecast reach is lifted by the median +109% observed across your 62 dual-platform posts.

Fair fee

fair fee = forecast views ÷ 1000 × target CPM. Default target is your portfolio median of $7.07; switch to $2.73 to buy only at top-quartile efficiency. Walk-away is set at 2× fair.

Scoring

Each metric is placed as a percentile against the campaigns in your file, split by platform. Views-per-follower carries the read because it is the only input that tracks cost efficiency (Spearman −0.69 against CPM). Consistency is the ratio of your weakest entered post to the median — it separates a reliable creator from one who hit the algorithm once. Engagement metrics are scored as an authenticity check only, against a fitted expectation curve (log₁₀(comments) = −1.66 + 0.669 × log₁₀(views) on TikTok), never as a value driver.

What this does not do

It does not read the profile for you — TikTok and Meta both block that from a browser page, and any tool claiming otherwise is either using a paid data vendor or guessing. It forecasts reach, not sales: your file records no conversion, promo-code or revenue data, so cost per view is as far as the evidence goes. Adding a sales column against these campaigns is the single highest-value thing you could do to this model.
