Six steps from a public profile to a fee you can defend in a negotiation. Every number the desk shows comes from this chain — nothing is hidden. The worked example follows one real creator profile end to end.

Read the reach the creator actually gets

Take the view count of their last five non-pinned posts and use the median, never the average. One viral post can be 10× their normal reach; an average lets them price off that outlier, a median does not.

median_views = median(v₁, v₂, v₃, v₄, v₅)
Worked example — @example.kol
Posts: 120K, 86K, 210K, 64K, 98K
Sorted: 64K, 86K, 98K, 120K, 210K
median_views98,000
Why this first: reach is the only thing you are really buying, and it is the only input that survives contact with the platform algorithm. Followers are a historical number; views are a current one.

Discount it to a sponsored post

A branded post reaches fewer people than an organic one — the audience scrolls past ads and the algorithm gives paid-looking content less push. Multiply by the sponsored reach factor.

forecast_views = median_views × 0.75 ← the dial in Assumptions
Continuing
98,000 × 0.7573,500
Be honest about this one: 0.75 is an industry convention, not something your 105 campaigns can prove — the file holds no organic baseline for the same creators to compare against. It is exposed as a dial for exactly that reason. Ask a creator for their last sponsored post's views and you can replace the assumption with a fact.

Add the cross-post if it is in the deal

Where a creator posted the same content to Facebook as well as TikTok, Facebook added a median +109% across your 62 dual-platform campaigns — for the same fee.

forecast_views = forecast_views × (1 + 1.09) ← if Facebook post contracted

Negotiation lever: this is the cheapest reach available to you. If a creator will not include the Facebook post, that is a price reduction, not a rounding error.

Convert reach into a fee

Price the forecast at the CPM you are willing to pay. CPM — cost per thousand views — is the media-buying unit that makes a 40K creator and a 2M creator directly comparable.

Score the five pillars against the book

Each pillar is a percentile against the campaigns in your file, on the matching platform. A score of 50 means "exactly typical for what HeBE has bought"; 80 means top fifth.

Cost efficiency CPM vs. book, inverted cheap = high Reach power views ÷ followers (VFR) Audience response engagement, shares, comments per view Reliability floor ratio (p25 ÷ median), spike-adjusted Trust & fit comment-curve residual + your checklist
Why five and not one: a single number hides why a creator failed. A cheap creator with a fake audience and a trustworthy creator who is overpriced both score badly — but you do completely different things about it. One you drop, the other you negotiate.

Weight the pillars by what the campaign is for

This is the step most brands skip. An awareness buy is won on cheap reach; a conversion buy is won on trust and audience fit. The same creator can be an A for one and a D for the other.

What this chain does not do.
  • It forecasts reach, not sales. Your file records no promo codes, clicks or revenue, so cost per view is as far as the evidence honestly goes.
  • It does not read profiles automatically. TikTok and Meta both block that from a web page — any tool that claims otherwise is paying a data vendor or guessing.
  • It assumes the creator's next post behaves like their last five. A creator who just changed content format, or bought followers last month, will read wrong.

Tier 2

Check the audience is real

These do not tell you who to book. They tell you who to disqualify — and that is a different job, which is why they are weighted as a gate, not a driver.

Engagement rate by view

ER-V
(likes + comments + shares) ÷ views

The correct engagement denominator on algorithmic feeds. Most agencies still quote engagement over followers, which on TikTok is close to meaningless — the audience that sees a post is mostly not the follower base.

Your bookMedian 8.3% · p25 6.0% · p90 16.9%
WarningIn your data ER-V runs against reach. Do not rank on it.

Comment rate

COMMENTS ÷ VIEWS
comments ÷ views, vs. expected for that view level

The hardest metric to fake and the best bot detector you have for free. Bought views and bought likes are cheap; a thread of real questions is not. Judge it against the level curve, since comment rates fall naturally as reach grows.

Expected≈10 comments at 10K views · 48 at 100K · 222 at 1M
Red flagUnder a third of expected — the desk raises "audience looks thin".

Share rate

SHARES ÷ VIEWS
shares ÷ views

The closest free proxy to intent. A like is a reflex; a share is someone putting their own name behind the product. On beauty, shares to private messages are the buying signal.

Your bookMedian 0.19% · p75 0.34% · p90 0.58%
GoodAbove 0.34% — weighted heavily on conversion campaigns.
Tier 3

What no dataset can tell you

Every experienced buyer checks these by eye before signing. They are the checklist in step 4 of the Evaluate tab, and they carry 30% of the score on a conversion campaign.

Audience geography

A Cambodian brand paying for a largely Thai or Vietnamese audience is buying nothing. Scan the comments — are they in Khmer? This is the most expensive silent failure in the category.

Category fit

A gaming creator posting a serum reads as an advert to their audience and converts accordingly. Jim Gaming: 2.5M followers, 2,256 views on a Mary&May eye cream, $132.98 CPM.

Sponsored saturation

Count branded posts in their last fifteen. Above roughly one in three, the audience has learned to scroll past and your post inherits that habit.

Competitor recency

A creator who posted for a rival skincare brand last month cannot credibly recommend yours. Sixty days is the usual exclusivity floor; write it into the contract.

Comment authenticity

Read twenty comments. Real audiences ask about price, shade and shipping. Bot audiences post emoji strings, "nice", and generic praise with no product noun in it.

Deal terms beyond the fee

Usage rights, whitelisting for paid amplification, and a re-post clause are worth real money and cost the creator little. A cheap fee with no usage rights is often the more expensive deal.

The gate check

Fourteen tests, run on every creator

Scoring tells you how good a creator is; gates tell you what is wrong. Each test is pass / caution / fail against a bar set for the creator's own follower tier, so a 2M account is not judged by a 40K account's standards. KEY marks the heaviest tests; FRAUD marks the authenticity checks.

G5, G6, G7, G8 and G9 are calibrated to your own campaigns — a creator passes when they beat the median of what HeBE actually bought at that follower tier, not a generic industry figure. Which metric leads, by objective

The most common expert mistake is applying one metric hierarchy to every campaign. These are the weights the Evaluate tab applies when you pick a goal.

The one finding that contradicts standard practice. Most agency decks rank creators by engagement rate. In your 105 campaigns, comment rate correlates +0.45 with a worse CPM, and share rate +0.22 — because engagement ratios are mechanically inflated on low-reach posts, where the denominator is small. Ranking on engagement rate systematically selects creators who cannot distribute. That is why this desk scores engagement as an authenticity gate and never as a value driver.
